About Baobab

A small Melbourne firm building household balance sheets the way accountants do — and clients can read.

Baobab started in 2018 because a senior accountant got tired of handing clients a forty-page PDF in July and watching them never open it. The plan was the work — but the artefact was wrong.

We rebuilt the planning workbook into something a household could open at the kitchen table. Twelve schedules. One file. The same numbers your accountant uses, with the linkages explained in plain English. The portal you’re reading is the latest version of that workbook — same logic, same sums, same FY 2025–26 ATO rates — turned into an interface that recalculates as you type.

We are deliberately small. Three planners, one technologist, one practice manager. We take on roughly forty new families a year. Each engagement is run by a senior planner who has been in the workbook for at least five financial years, and reviewed quarterly by the practice partner.

We are not a robo-adviser. We do not sell products. We do not take asset-based fees or commissions. We charge a flat annual fee for the planning work, and we walk it with you four times a year. If your accountant or your lender or your conveyancer needs the numbers, they get a JSON export and the workbook walks itself.

A short history

Seven years.

  1. 2018

    Mai leaves a top-tier firm to start Baobab.

    A practice that does the planning work end-to-end, doesn’t sell products, and uses one workbook per family. The first version is an Excel file with eight tabs and two clients.

  2. 2020

    The workbook expands to twelve schedules.

    Cashflow calendar, rental schedule and goals tab added. Diego joins. The practice has thirty households and a waitlist for the first time.

  3. 2022

    Authorised Representative status under our AFSL.

    We bring the planning work in-house under a tighter compliance frame. Quarterly reviews become standard for every tier.

  4. 2024

    The workbook becomes a portal.

    What was an Excel hand-off is rebuilt as a private dashboard. Same sums, same logic, but live, persistent and reviewable on a phone.

  5. 2026

    One hundred and eighty-seven households.

    Capacity is capped on purpose at ≈ forty new families per year so the partner stays on every file. The waitlist is currently four months.
